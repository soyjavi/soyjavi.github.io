COPY-NO-AICopy · vocabulary

Say my clone, never AI

Why. The creator does not want the site to say AI. It says it in 33 places and in the share image, calls the offer a gadget, and the one thing it is for, people who knew him being able to keep talking with him, appears nowhere.

Accept a test greps content/, src/, design/, README.md, SPEC.md, ROADMAP.md, AGENTS.md and the built pages, case-insensitively, for the banned words in both languages and finds nothing, except the memory the creator decides to keep; the share image is regenerated; ROADMAP IDs CLONE-NAME and CLONE-SPEC keep their names.

COPY-HEROCopy · hero

A hero that says who, what and what comes next, in 45 words

Why. The first card a visitor reads is a manual: it explains clouds and galaxies before saying what the site is for, and it still names an AI. The instructions already live in the guide and the hint. Two shorter ledes, no AI, each naming both offers.

Accept hero.lede is at most 45 words in English and Spanish, names the book and the clone, contains none of the banned words and no time promise; the hero keeps its form and links at the six sizes already tested; the clone link reads “About my clone →” / “Sobre mi clon →”.

COPY-BOOKCopy · the book

For my children, and for whoever wants it

Why. The book is for his children and for anyone who wants to read it, and it will hold his life, what he learnt, what went well and what went wrong. The card says only the first half, promises a date, and tells a story of the writing that the creator has not given.

Accept book.body in both languages names his children and whoever wants it and carries the pair “what went well and what went wrong” / “lo que salió bien y lo que salió mal”; nothing in content/ matches soon, coming, muy pronto or próximamente; the empty state says the list is not open yet; the creator confirms every statement about the book, in particular whether “I’m writing it” is true.

COPY-CLONECopy · the clone

A place to keep talking, when I am no longer here

Why. The clone is the delicate one: it is for whoever wants to keep talking with him when he is no longer in this world, especially those who knew him, and it grows from the idea he built as Clonara. The card calls it an AI, says anyone, and says nothing of why. Three framings, each saying it is not ready and promising nothing it can do.

Accept clone.title, body, button and note are rewritten in both languages and carry “no longer here” / “ya no esté” and “isn’t ready yet” / “todavía no está listo”, and none of “based on”, “anyone”, “forever”, “answer”; the nav, the kicker, the hud and the guide say “My clone” / “Mi clon”; the contact title no longer says talk; the creator confirms whether to keep “an ambitious project” and whether to mention the idea it grows from; nothing presents it as existing.

COPY-WAITLISTCopy · lists and invitations

Softer words for the lists and the nudge

Why. Every promise of time and every product word on the lists comes from the same place: “very soon”, “join the waitlist”, “wait for the AI”. They are small strings and they set the tone of the invitation, so they get one pass together.

Accept no string in content/ promises a time; the nudge, the hero link and the form labels carry no banned word; the Spanish verb forms agree with “clon”; the form still never claims that a signup succeeded; the creator decides whether the nudge may appear on the memories about loss.

COPY-METACopy · search and sharing

What a search result and a shared link say

Why. The description is what appears under the link in a search and when it is pasted, and today it says AI twice, “personal site” and 171 characters. The 404 uses a vocabulary the site does not have.

Accept meta.description is at most 155 characters in both languages, has no banned word, no “personal site” and no “milestones”, and is the same text in the description, og:description and twitter:description; the 404 title, heading and link use the sky, not days; og-image.png is regenerated after this change.

COPY-VOICECopy · voice and rules

One voice, twelve rules, nine Spanish fixes

Why. The memories, the period intros, the contact and the 404 share a voice: first person, plain, short, a little dry. The hero and the clone break it, the interface mixes three vocabularies (milestones, memories, days) and the Spanish has a handful of calques. A reader who knew him lands on the same hero as a stranger, and nothing says the clone is for them.

Accept the twelve rules are written in design/index.html’s voice section and a test enforces the mechanical ones (banned words, time promises, key parity, quotes, no ‘ratón’ or ‘Saluda’); the Spanish fixes are applied; the creator chooses the tense of the memories and approves every sentence about the clone.

BRAND-NAMEBrand · name

javi, or soyjavi: which one is the brand

Why. The creator wants the name worked properly before anything is drawn again. Six candidates, each in both lights with what it gains and what it costs, scored on warmth, dignity (a book for his children, a conversation that outlives him), findability, how it sounds, consistency with the handle and clarity in Spanish and English; scores are judgement, not data.

Accept the creator picks one (or names another) and says whether the book’s byline is a first name or a full name. Then the wordmark, the monogram, the title tags, the JSON-LD (name, alternateName, a WebSite node), the author meta and the copy follow in one change, the brand page and its tests move with it, and the five checks are done with real people before launch: dictation (“javi dot com”, ten Spanish and ten English speakers), a five-second header test (portfolio, memorial or blog?), a read by people who have lost someone, findability after launch and the favicon among twenty tabs. Nothing ships before the pick.

BRAND-WORDMARKBrand · wordmark

Eight ways to draw the name, and the two worth keeping

Why. The wordmark is the one drawn thing on the site, so it earns a careful look. Eight treatments with Instrument Serif, Geist and Geist Mono (the only families the repository may use), judged by eye in both lights; the winner is the one that already ships, and the header does not use it.

Accept the wordmark in the header is the outlined SVG (inline, currentColor, 28 px high, 24 px under 640 px) and no live text spells the brand; a test compares its bounding box with the gutter and its baseline with the nav labels; the outlined files are generated from the font by a tool and a test fails when they drift; the brand page shows the chosen treatment and the runner-up.

BRAND-SMALLBrand · small sizes

The mark at 16 px, the home-screen icon and the share card

Why. The mark has to be recognised among twenty tabs and on a phone’s home screen, and today it is thinnest exactly there. The share image, the first thing anyone sees when the link is pasted, is from the previous version of the site.

Accept the favicon reads as a j at 16, 32 and 180 px in both themes (a screenshot test counts ink pixels); an apple-touch icon and a 32 px PNG are linked from the head; og-image.png is regenerated from the current home, and a test fails when its source nav or title no longer matches the page.

BRAND-TYPE-SCALEBrand · type

A named scale, and nothing structural below 12 px

Why. The things that carry the idea, a galaxy’s name and a memory’s tag, are the smallest and dimmest type on the screen: 10 px muted mono and a 15 px hairline serif over a glow. The stylesheet has no scale: sizes from 9 to 40 px and five different tracking values for what is one style.

Accept brand.css names the sizes and the tracking as tokens and a test fails on any font-size or letter-spacing literal outside them, as it does for colour; a browser test walks the visible text at 1280×640 and 390×844 and finds nothing under 11 px (12 px for muted mono over the scene); no serif is drawn under 17 px (16 px on a phone); the galaxy names clear 7 to 1 against a background sampled from the rendered scene. This replaces the label sizes shipped with ONE-LABEL-VOICE.

BRAND-LOCKUPBrand · the two offers

One author, two works: by javi, with javi

Why. A book is by someone and a conversation is with someone, so two small prepositions can tie both offers to the one name without a tool word. The clone is the delicate one: it is for people who miss him, so its name must be honest about what it is and never claim to be him.

Accept the book’s card and the clone’s card show the same lockup at the same place (a mono preposition and the outlined wordmark at 18 to 22 px); the word AI and its Spanish twin IA appear nowhere in content/, src/ or the built pages; the clone’s card carries one sentence of disclosure in both languages; the Buttondown tag and the dictionary key names stay internal; the creator picks the clone’s public name and the repository holds no name for it until then.

KIND-TINTScene · memories

Show the kind of each memory

Why. Every memory has a kind (personal, professional, product, education) but every cloud is one ink, so the kind is only in the card. A faint tint per kind (A) would show it at a glance, but it breaks the identity's rule of no hue and every colour test, so it needs an explicit exception; a small mark beside the cloud (B) keeps one ink.

Accept the creator decides. A needs the identity rule rewritten (AGENTS, the brand page, the colour tests) and tints that keep labels at 4.5 to 1; B needs none. Either way it is a toggle (“Show kinds”, off by default), a four-line legend names the kinds, and the kind stays in the card's text so colour or shape is never the only cue.

PORTRAIT-DOTSScene · a station

The avatar as a dot portrait

Why. The masked figure of the avatar says more than a person without a word. Drawn with the same fine dots as the sky, in the card of a station of its own, it becomes part of the world instead of a picture pasted on it.

Accept a station “Who” after the opening whose card holds the avatar sampled into at most a thousand dots drawn in the ink of the theme (no hue), with the same title reveal as any card; it is in the document as real text and an alt, keyboard-reachable, and in the flat page as the avatar itself. A pure function samples the image at build time and a test pins the dot count.

MILESTONE-MEDIACard

A photo or a sound at a milestone

Why. A few memories are better with a photograph or a recording. In the card a photo is drawn in dots first and sharpens on press; a sound shows its waveform and plays only when asked. Only where the creator has approved it.

Accept a memory file may name one local image and/or one audio file, both optional and public by approval; the image carries an alt, nothing loads before the card opens and nothing leaves the origin, sound never autoplays, the flat page shows the image and an audio control, and a test fails on any media for a memory not marked public.

PRIVATE-EDITIONFamily · outside the repository

A family-only edition

Why. The same sky built from the private archive for the creator's children, with the held-back memories added and a banner saying it is not published. It would be built locally from the same code and never enter this repository.

Accept an npm run family that reads the private archive from a path outside the repository and builds a separate folder with the same engine and its own banner; nothing of it is ever written inside the repository, its history, a test, a fixture or a doc; the public build is byte-identical with or without the command.

DEPTH-LOCKEDScene · depth

Show how much more there is than what is public

Why. A visitor sees only the public memories and cannot feel the depth behind them. Fine hollow dots for what is kept for the book, unnamed, with one line when pressed, let them feel it without anything being said about what those memories hold.

Accept the number of hollow dots is the one the creator chooses to publish (a single number in content/site.json), placed by period with a seed and carrying no title, date or fact; pressing one says it is in the book and links to the waitlist; nothing about a held-back memory is in the repository, a test or a doc.

BOOK-CHAPTERSScene · the book

The book as nodes

Why. The book is one ring today. As a run of chapter rings along the path ahead, each opens a card with its blurb and the memories it draws on, which are lit in every galaxy.

Accept chapters are listed in content/ in both languages with a title, a blurb and public memory ids, and appear only once the creator provides them (the repository holds no chapter title before); each ring is a station with its own card and lines to its memories, in the rail and the keyboard order, and the book's ring stays the last.

RELATIONS-VIEWScene · a view

A view of who and what was connected

Why. A memory shows its own links, but not the web of them. A view that draws every link of the life at once, the clouds dimmed, shows which memories hold the rest together; it matters once there are hundreds.

Accept a toggle (key R and a button in the sheet) that draws every declared link as a hairline arc and dims the clouds, with the line count in the corner; a memory in view lights its own links as usual; off by default, and capped so that 300 memories and 600 links stay at the frame rate of the quality tier.

DREAM-ARCSScene · what was wished for

What was wished for, done and let go

Why. Some memories are the place where something was wished for and others where it came true. A dashed arc from one to the other, and a hollow ring for what is still pending, tells that story without a sentence.

Accept an optional wish field in a memory pointing at the memory where it came true (or pending), only for dreams the creator makes public; arcs draw in the sky when the wish memory is open, pending ones are hollow rings like the book and the AI, and a test fails on a pointer to a memory that does not exist.

SKY-DOMEScene · space

A deep field behind the galaxies

Why. On a large or high-density screen the night is almost empty: a few faint specks, because the stars are only a few thousand points and most are too dim to see. A deep field baked once into a texture at load (thousands of stars of every magnitude, a soft band of density, a few haloed ones) costs nothing per frame, fills the sky at any resolution and leaves the galaxies as the only named things.

Accept at load the scene bakes an equirectangular canvas of at least 4096 px (seeded, so the sky is always the same) and draws it on an inside-out sphere behind everything; at 2000 px wide on a retina screen at least a thousand stars are visible at the whole-life view, none brighter than the clouds, in neutral ink only; the band follows the star field's tilt; it is one draw call, free on the lowest quality tier, and a unit test pins the star count, the magnitude curve and that the seed is stable.

SPACE-HAZEScene · space

A faint haze of dust

Why. Flat black makes the galaxies look pasted on. A very faint, neutral haze drifting a little slower than the galaxies gives the space volume and the camera something to move against, with no hue.

Accept a grayscale noise haze (baked or shader) at no more than 8% of the ink, scrolling with the camera at a lower rate than the galaxies; never a hue, never over text; absent on the lowest quality tier and under reduced motion; a test pins the maximum alpha.

FAR-GALAXIESScene · space

Distant galaxies that are not memories

Why. A few dozen tiny, dim spirals very far behind remind the visitor how small a life is without competing with it. They are never clickable and never named.

Accept between 20 and 60 smudges drawn once into the deep field, each under 3% of the screen height and under 40% of a cloud's brightness, with no label and no picking; a test fails if any is picked or named.

GALAXY-GLOWScene · galaxies

A soft glow around every galaxy

Why. Dense periods and sparse ones look alike from far away. A soft additive glow around each galaxy, in proportion to what it holds, tells at a glance where more happened and lifts the whole-life view.

Accept one sprite per galaxy, additive in the night theme and a faint ink wash in the paper theme, sized by the galaxy's radius and strength by its memory count; it dims with a filter like the clouds; capped at 15% of the ink; a test checks the strength follows the count.

SHOOTING-STARSScene · life in the background

A slow light that crosses the sky

Why. A rare, slow streak of light crossing the background says the sky is alive without asking for attention: calm, like the rest.

Accept one streak every 40 to 90 seconds on the whole-life view only, slower than a second and a half, at most 30% of the ink, never over the card or the controls, not while anything is open or the tour runs, off under reduced motion, in the paper theme and on the lowest quality tier; a test with a faked clock checks the interval and that none appears at a memory.

PAPER-GRAINBrand · the paper theme

The paper theme with its own sky

Why. In the light theme the background is a flat paper with a few specks, so the stars of the night have no counterpart. A faint grain of fibres and ink specks gives the paper a sky of its own and the same sense of depth.

Accept a seeded grain baked once (fibres and ink specks, none above 25% of the ink, denser near the edges), drawn behind the scene only in the paper theme; contrast of every text pair still at least 4.5 to 1 against the grain (the existing colour test), no hue, and the switch repaints it without a reload.
